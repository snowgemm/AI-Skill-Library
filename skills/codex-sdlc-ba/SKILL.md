# Business Analysis

Use when understanding a business problem, clarifying stakeholder needs, documenting workflows, or translating requirements for a delivery team.

## Workflow

1. Identify stakeholders, decision owners, affected users, and the problem or opportunity to solve.
2. Map the current process and pain points using evidence, not assumptions about how users behave.
3. Define future-state outcomes, business rules, actors, data inputs/outputs, exceptions, and constraints.
4. Write requirements with unique identifiers, priority, rationale, acceptance criteria, and traceability to goals.
5. Capture non-functional needs: privacy, security, accessibility, performance, reliability, retention, and auditability.
6. Review conflicting requirements and unresolved questions with the appropriate stakeholder.
7. Map requirements to implementation tasks and test cases; update traceability when scope changes.
8. Do not invent stakeholder decisions or treat unvalidated assumptions as approved requirements.

## Verification checklist

- Stakeholders and problem are identified.
- Current and future workflows are mapped.
- Requirements are testable and traceable.
- Exceptions and non-functional constraints are included.
- Assumptions are distinguished from approvals.

## Reporting

Report source context, decisions, work items, evidence, tests, limitations, and ownership. Do not treat a planning or coordination document as proof that implementation or release work occurred.
