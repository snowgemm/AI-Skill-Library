# Market and Opportunity Sizing

Use when estimating the scale of a market, segment, customer base, or opportunity, or comparing growth scenarios.

## Workflow

1. Define the product/category, geography, customer segment, time horizon, currency, and whether the estimate is annual spend, units, revenue, or users.
2. Choose a sizing method that fits the evidence: top-down market reports, bottom-up customers × spend, value-based usage, or a hybrid triangulation.
3. Write the equation and every assumption explicitly. Separate observed inputs from estimates and proxies.
4. Use multiple independent sources where possible. Check dates, geographic coverage, category definitions, and whether sources overlap.
5. Build base, low, and high scenarios. Sensitize the assumptions that contribute most to uncertainty.
6. Distinguish TAM, SAM, and SOM using realistic product, channel, geographic, capacity, and competitive constraints.
7. Check arithmetic, prevent double counting, and clearly state why the estimate should or should not drive a decision.

## Verification checklist

- Market definition and time horizon are explicit.
- Sources and dates are recorded.
- Bottom-up assumptions are inspectable.
- Ranges and sensitivities are shown.
- TAM/SAM/SOM are not conflated.

## Reporting

State the source and version of data/artifacts, filters and definitions, checks performed, assumptions, unresolved limitations, and files delivered. Do not imply the host has connected data, publication, sharing, scheduling, or file-conversion capabilities unless those tools are actually available and report success.
